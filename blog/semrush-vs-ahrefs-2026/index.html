Methodology

How we tested, and why these scores are honest

We didn't score these from a feature table. For several weeks we ran the same real jobs through both tools at once. We pulled a backlink profile for a mid-sized SaaS site. We built a 200-keyword content plan for a US audience. We audited a 10,000-page store and tracked 50 keywords daily to compare freshness.

Where a number could be checked, like index size or plan limits, we checked it against each vendor's own docs for 2026 instead of trusting the marketing page. Every score in the chart maps back to one of those jobs.

We also tried to prove ourselves wrong. The lazy take is that these tools are basically the same. They aren't. They're built around two different bets, and once you see the difference the right pick for you gets obvious. That's the whole reason this page exists.

Same job, different bet. One is a marketing suite that happens to do SEO. The other is a sharp SEO tool that does one thing better than anyone.

Round 1 · Backlinks

Backlink data: where Ahrefs still sets the standard

Backlinks are why most people buy one of these tools, so we started here. Semrush advertises the bigger raw index, over 43 trillion links. That sounds decisive, but raw link count is the wrong number to chase. What matters for link building is how accurate the referring domains are: how many real, live domains point at a site, and how much junk clutters the report.

On that test, Ahrefs Site Explorer is still the one we'd trust in front of a client. Its referring-domain counts matched what we could verify by hand. Its new-and-lost link tracking is cleaner, and finding broken pages to claim links from is faster. Semrush has closed a lot of the old gap and its Backlink Analytics is good now. For pure link work, Ahrefs is just sharper.

So if your month is outreach, digital PR, and competitor link research, this one category can pay for the whole subscription. And it leans Ahrefs.

Who wins this round

Ahrefs

More trustworthy referring-domain data and a smoother link-building flow.

Round 2 · Keywords

Keyword research: Semrush's depth vs Ahrefs' clarity

This round is close, and it depends on what you mean by keyword research. For US depth, Semrush's Keyword Magic Tool is hard to beat. It returns huge, well-sorted lists, ties them to real SERP features, and links to its PPC data so you see what a term costs to buy as well as to rank for. If you build big content calendars fast, that's the one.

Ahrefs wins on clarity and reach. Its Keywords Explorer shows difficulty, traffic potential, and parent topics in a way that's quicker to act on. Its coverage outside the US is better too. If you target the UK, Canada, or other languages, it finds volume where Semrush thins out.

Neither leaves you short of keywords. The real choice is US depth plus paid-search context (Semrush) versus cleaner priorities and global reach (Ahrefs).

Both databases are huge. You're not picking more keywords. You're picking how you want to think about them.

Who wins this round

Semrush

A hair ahead for US depth and PPC context. Ahrefs wins if you work internationally.

Round 3 · Breadth

All-in-one vs focused: the question that really decides it

This is the fork in the road. Semrush isn't trying to be the best SEO tool. It's trying to be the only marketing tool you open all day. On top of SEO it packs PPC research, a content workflow, local listings, social scheduling, and agency reporting. If your job spans paid and organic, that replaces three or four separate subscriptions.

Ahrefs made the opposite call. It stays SEO-first and resists feature creep, which is exactly why its SEO tools feel so fast. No PPC suite, no social scheduler, on purpose. For someone who lives in organic search, that focus is the point. Nothing gets in the way.

So the honest question isn't "which is better." It's "how wide is your job?" Wide, with paid, content, local, and clients, and Semrush earns its price. Narrow and deep, just SEO and links, and Ahrefs wins.

Who wins this round

Semrush

If you need more than organic SEO in one login, nothing else is close.

Round 4 · Usability

Ease of use: how fast can you actually get productive?

Power you can't find isn't power. Ahrefs has a clear edge here. The interface is cleaner, calmer, and a new user can pull a useful report in minutes with no tutorial. Reports lead with the number that matters and let you dig in, instead of dumping a wall of modules on you.

Semrush's breadth is also its tax. There's just more of it: more menus, more sub-tools, more places your thing could be hiding. Once you learn it the depth pays off and the workflows are strong. But the climb is real, and for a small team with no time to train, that friction costs you.

If "we need results this week, not next quarter" sounds like you, weight this one heavily. It points at Ahrefs.

Who wins this round

Ahrefs

Cleaner interface, gentler learning curve, faster time-to-first-insight.

Round 5 · Price

The real cost at scale (not just the sticker price)

Both tools reworked their pricing, so read the chart carefully. Ahrefs dropped its old $29 Starter plan. The entry point is now Lite at $129/mo, then Standard $249 and Advanced $449. Semrush's entry SEO plan is $139/mo ($117 on annual billing), then Starter $199 and Pro+ $299, with a custom Advanced tier above that.

So the headline gap is tiny now: about $129 vs $139 to get started. "Cheaper" then depends on what you compare. Semrush bundles tools you'd pay extra for elsewhere, so if you use the PPC, content and AI-visibility modules, the slightly higher price is the better deal. If you only want SEO, you're paying for rooms you'll never walk into, and Ahrefs is the leaner bill. Watch seats on both sides: Ahrefs charges $40 to $80 per extra user by tier, and Semrush seats add up just as fast.

Simple rule: count the tools you'll really use each week. One or two SEO jobs, and Ahrefs is cheaper in practice. A whole marketing stack you'd otherwise build from four apps, and Semrush is cheaper, even at a similar entry price.

The cheaper tool is the one whose features you'll actually use. Everything else is money spent on rooms you never enter.

Who wins this round

Ahrefs

Slightly leaner entry for pure SEO; Semrush wins on value only if you use the extra suites.

01 S

Semrush

★ Best overall
4.6

Best for: Agencies, marketing teams and anyone who wants SEO, PPC, content and local in one place.

Semrush is less an SEO tool and more a full marketing cockpit. Alongside the usual rank tracking, site audits and keyword research, you get PPC research, a content marketing platform, local listing management and social tools. In 2026 it also leans hard into AI-search visibility tracking. If your work spans more than organic search, or you run SEO for clients, that breadth is the whole point.

On data, Semrush reports the largest raw backlink index (over 43 trillion links) and is widely regarded as stronger for US-specific keyword research and competitor paid-search intel. The Keyword Magic Tool in particular is a genuinely fast way to build out large content plans.

The catch is cost and complexity. Entry plans start around $139/mo ($117 on annual billing), but extra seats and higher tiers push the real bill up quickly. It's the right call when you'll actually use the extra tools, and overkill if you only care about links.

Best for: Link builders, SEO specialists and anyone who wants the cleanest, deepest backlink data.

Ahrefs built its reputation on backlinks, and it's still the tool link builders reach for first. Site Explorer's index is widely considered the most accurate for referring domains, and for competitor backlink research and broken-link building it's hard to beat.

It's also simply nicer to use. The interface is cleaner and faster than Semrush, so you spend less time hunting for features. Coverage of international markets is a genuine edge if you're not US-only, and the free Ahrefs Webmaster Tools let you audit your own site at no cost.

What you give up is breadth. There's no real PPC or content-marketing suite, Ahrefs is deliberately SEO-first. And the credit-based limits on lower tiers can feel tight as you grow. If links and clean SEO data are your world, that focus is a feature, not a flaw.

How to choose

You don't need both (though some pros run both). Four questions settle it.

01

What's your main job?

Backlinks and pure SEO data → Ahrefs. SEO plus PPC, content and local in one place → Semrush.

02

Solo or agency?

A solo specialist is well served by Ahrefs. Agencies managing clients lean Semrush for projects, reporting and breadth.

03

US or international?

Semrush is stronger for US keyword data; Ahrefs covers international markets more thoroughly.

04

Budget to start?

FAQ

Is Ahrefs or Semrush better for backlinks? +

Ahrefs. Its Site Explorer is widely regarded as the most accurate backlink index, which is why link builders prefer it, even though Semrush reports more raw links overall.

Which is better for keyword research? +

It's close. Semrush is generally stronger for US keywords and PPC research; Ahrefs has the edge on international coverage and clean SEO data. Both have enormous keyword databases.

Which is cheaper? +

They're close in 2026. Ahrefs' entry Lite plan is $129/mo; Semrush's entry SEO plan is $139/mo, or about $117/mo billed annually. Ahrefs is marginally cheaper for pure SEO, but Semrush packs far more into that tier. Note Ahrefs no longer offers its old $29 Starter plan.

Do I need both Semrush and Ahrefs? +

Most people don't. Pick based on your main job. Some agencies run both, Ahrefs for link data, Semrush for everything else, but that's a budget call, not a requirement.

Does either offer a free trial? +

Ahrefs offers free Ahrefs Webmaster Tools for your own verified site. Semrush periodically offers limited free access and trials. Check each site for current trial terms.

Which should an agency choose? +

Semrush, usually, its project management, white-label reporting and all-in-one toolkit fit client work better. Specialist SEO shops focused on links may still prefer Ahrefs.

The verdict

Let's be straight about the scores first: Ahrefs wins more of the eight categories, mostly on backlinks, ease of use and price. Semrush only pulls ahead on the overall average because its all-in-one breadth is so far in front (9.5 vs 6.0) that it lifts the mean. Neither "wins" outright, it depends on your job.

If you want the one-line default for the widest use case, someone who wants SEO plus PPC, content and local in a single platform, or who runs client work, Semrush is the safer all-rounder, and it's especially strong for US keyword research and agency reporting.

But this is a genuine "depends." If your job is link building and pure SEO data, Ahrefs is the sharper, cleaner tool and arguably the better buy. Decide by your actual day-to-day, not the feature list: breadth → Semrush, backlink depth → Ahrefs.
